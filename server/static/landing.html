Real-time collaboration for KiCad

Real-time collaboration built into the schematic and PCB editors. You see each other's cursors, edits land as they are made, and the project lives online, so nobody has to pass a zip around. It works in the desktop app and in the browser.

How a board gets made

Sheet 1 / 5

One design, from the first wire to a finished board, in a file everyone has open. Scroll to follow it.

  1. 1. Schematic

    Draw the circuit together

    Symbols, wires and labels show up on everyone's sheet as they are placed. A wire someone is still drawing appears as a preview, so two people never wire the same net twice.

  2. 2. Placement

    Split the board and place it

    Parts arrive on the board in a pile, with airwires showing what connects to what. Take the connector while someone else takes the timing parts. A part being dragged is outlined in its owner's colour.

  3. 3. Routing

    Route it, by hand or not

    Tracks appear for everyone segment by segment as they are laid. In the web editor, the autorouter can finish the connections you leave and shows its result as a preview before anything is kept.

  4. 4. 3D viewer

    It is still KiCad

    The files are ordinary .kicad_sch and .kicad_pcb. Rule checks and the 3D viewer work in the browser too, and fabrication outputs come from the desktop app as they always have. Name a checkpoint before you order, and restore it if you need to.

Working together

Sheet 2 / 5

What you see when someone else is in the file.

  1. Named cursors and selectionsEveryone gets a colour. Their pointer and whatever they have selected are drawn in it on your canvas.
  2. Edits before they landA track being routed, a wire being drawn or a part being dragged shows as a preview until it is committed.
  3. FollowFollow a collaborator's viewport while they walk you through a design.
  4. CommentsPin a comment to a spot on the canvas, or keep a thread on the document.
  5. CheckpointsName a version. The project's owner can restore it later.
  6. OfflineLose the connection and keep working. Edits are journalled locally and replayed when you are back.

Sheet 4 / 5

Use the desktop app or the browser. It is the same project either way.

Desktop app

All of KiCad, with collaboration built into the editors. Use it when you need the full toolset.

  1. Open the project manager and choose File, Online Projects.
  2. Sign in with GitHub. Upload a project or open one shared with you.
  3. Use Share to invite someone, then open the same sheet or board.

Browser

Nothing to install. Good for a review, a quick fix from another machine, or a collaborator who does not have KiCad.

  1. Sign in with GitHub.
  2. Upload a KiCad project, import one from a repository, or open an invite link.
  3. Edit. People in the desktop app see you, and you see them.

How it works

Sheet 5 / 5

The editors send property-level changes over a WebSocket to a Rust server backed by PostgreSQL. The server puts every operation in order and stores it before acknowledging it and passing it on, so everyone applies the same edits in the same sequence.

When two people change the same property, the last writer wins. Cursors and selections travel on a separate channel that is never stored.

It is open source, and the server is yours to run: self-hosting guide, collaboration guide.
